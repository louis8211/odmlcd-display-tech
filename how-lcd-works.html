How an LCD Works: From Backlight to the Image You Specify

Fundamentals · Companion to the article on odmlcd.com

A liquid crystal display does not emit light. It controls light. Understanding the stack of layers between the backlight and your eye is the fastest way to make sense of every spec on a TFT LCD module datasheet — brightness, contrast, viewing angle and response time all originate here.

The layer stack, bottom to top

  • Backlight — LEDs behind the panel provide the only light source. Brightness (nits) and uniformity are set here, not by the liquid crystals.
  • Bottom polariser — filters the backlight into a single linear polarisation.
  • TFT glass with liquid crystals — each pixel is a tiny cell whose crystals twist or align under voltage, rotating polarisation to control how much light passes.
  • Colour filter (RGB sub-pixels) — three sub-pixels per pixel produce the full colour image.
  • Top polariser — crossed relative to the bottom one, so "closed" crystals block light and "open" ones pass it.

What "TFT" actually drives

A thin-film transistor sits at every pixel as a fast, stable switch. That is what gives TFT LCDs their sharpness and response time versus older passive-matrix panels. The transistor array is addressed by a timing controller (TCON) that receives the video signal from the controller board.
